Wobble Isle

Privacy policy

Last updated 10 October 2026

Wobble Isle asks for no email, password or contacts, shows no ads and does not track you. You pick a player name, which other players see, so don’t use your real name. To play online the game uses an anonymous player ID. You can delete all of it from inside the game at any time.

Wobble Isle is a party game for iPhone made by E2 PARTNERS L.L.C-FZ (“we”). This page says what the game collects, why, who else handles it, how long it is kept, and how to delete it.

What the game keeps on your phone

Your player name, your points and level, and the players you have blocked. Your choices: your character, the mode, how well the bots play, and the mini-games you picked. Also a random number that lets a room give you back your seat if your connection drops, and the sign-in for online play. They stay on your phone, except as described below.

What online play uses

Playing against bots needs no connection and sends nothing. When you host, join or look for a room:

  • An anonymous player ID. Unity Authentication makes it the first time your phone goes online. It is not linked to your real name, your email or your Apple ID.
  • Your IP address. Unity’s servers see it when your phone connects, as any internet service does, and use it to carry the game between the phones in a room and to pick a server near you, which tells them roughly where you are: your region, never your precise location.
  • Your player name. The name you pick, checked by a word filter. When you join a room it goes to the room’s host, who passes it on to everyone in the room, and they see it on their screens.
  • The game itself. Your moves, your character and the room’s settings travel between the phones in the room while you play. So does your player ID, which lets the others’ phones report your name or block you, and count the game for the world rank. They are not stored.
  • Open rooms. A room its host lets anyone join is listed with its code, its games and settings, the host’s player name and the host’s anonymous player ID, so that other players can find it. The listing is removed when the room closes.
  • The world rank. When an online game with other people begins and when it ends, your phone tells our server who sat where (by player ID), how the game went, who left it early, and your player name and points. From level 10 the server rates you against the other people in the game, keeps your rating, the games it counted and how many you played with each person that day, and shows your player name and rating on the world rank lists.
  • Reports. If you report a player’s name, we keep who reported whom, so that we can look into it. A name several players report can be taken off the world rank lists.
  • Error reports. If one of Unity’s online services runs into a problem, it may send Unity a technical report about it, not linked to you.

We do not collect real names, email addresses, phone numbers, contacts, photos, precise location, advertising identifiers or payment details. The game has no chat and no ads, and nothing in it tracks you across other apps or websites. We run no analytics of our own; Unity says its leaderboard service may use player IDs to measure how the service is used.

Why

Only to run online play: to sign your phone in, connect you to rooms, give you back your seat after a dropped connection, show open rooms, show who is playing, keep the world rank fair, and look into names players report. Never for advertising or profiles, and never sold.

Who else handles it

Unity Technologies runs the online services for us: Unity Authentication, Unity Relay, Unity Lobby, and, for the world rank, Unity Cloud Code, Unity Cloud Save and Unity Leaderboards. Unity handles the data on our behalf under its data processing terms, which require it to protect the data at least as well as this policy does. How Unity handles data is in Unity’s privacy policy. Unity’s servers are in several countries, including the United States.

Other players in a room see your player name, your character and how you play, and their phones receive your player ID. Anyone playing the game can see the names and ratings on the world rank lists, and the player name of the host of an open room. Nobody else receives your data from us.

How long it is kept

  • On your phone: until you delete it, or delete the game.
  • Your player ID at Unity: until you delete it from the game, or ask us to.
  • Unity Relay and Unity Lobby: IP addresses and player IDs in their records for up to 30 days.
  • Open-room listings: until the room closes.
  • A game’s report for the world rank: about an hour after the game is settled.
  • Your rating, your place on the world rank lists and your reports of names: until you delete your data.

Deleting your data

In the game, tap the round button at the top left of the first screen, then Delete my data. It deletes your rating, your rows on the world rank lists, the reports you made and the reports of your name, your player ID at Unity and everything the game keeps on your phone, and the game starts afresh as on a new phone.

You can also write to us with your player ID, shown at the bottom left of that page, and we will delete it. Deleting the game from your phone removes what it kept there, but not your player ID at Unity: use Delete my data first. You can stop sharing anything with us at any time by playing only against bots.

Children

The game has no chat, no ads and no purchases. Player names are checked by a word filter, and any player can report a name or block a player. The game uses player names, the anonymous player ID and the IP address only to run online play, never to contact anyone, to advertise or to build profiles. If you think a child has sent us anything more, write to us and we will delete it.

Your rights

Depending on where you live, you may have the right to see, correct or delete your data, or to object to how it is used. We hold no real names or contact details, so we find your data by your player ID: write to us with it. You can change your player name in the game at any time. You can also complain to your data protection authority.

Changes

When this policy changes, this page and its date change with it.
